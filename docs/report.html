Finding 01 · Timing

The peak is at six, not five-twenty.

Your sampling grid clusters tightly around 16:30–17:30 — four separate marks inside one hour. The actual Friday peak lands a full forty minutes later, at 18:00, and the 6pm hour carries more rides than any other moment of the week.

Rides per day, by hour

Saturday is not a quieter day — it carries more total rides than Friday (37,670 vs 37,326), but spreads them across an afternoon that peaks at 16:00. Weekend scarcity is a different shape entirely, not a smaller version of the weekday one.

Finding 02 · Direction

Manhattan empties. Williamsburg fills.

Net flow inside any single half-hour is small — three or four bikes against a fifty-dock station. That's why it can't explain an empty rack on its own. What does is the accumulated flow across the whole day: a station bleeding two bikes an hour from 7am is twenty down by evening.

Measured that way, the pattern is unambiguous. Five of the six deepest drains sit in the Lower East Side. Three of the six deepest pile-ups sit in Williamsburg proper.

Peak accumulated pressure, as a share of the station's own capacity

Friday, accumulated from 05:00. Negative means bikes leave faster than they arrive; positive means they pile up. Scaled by capacity, because 26 bikes into a 28-dock station is a very different event from 30 into a 117-dock station.

N 6 St & Bedford Ave is the sharpest case in the system: a 28-dock station absorbing a net 26 bikes by 20:45 — 93% of everything it can hold. If you are trying to dock near the Bedford L on a Friday night, that is the station working against you.

Finding 03 · Shape

Each station has a signature.

The curves below trace accumulated pressure from 5am to midnight. The drains bottom out in the early afternoon and stay down; the fills climb all evening. Their timing is as useful as their depth — it tells you the hour a station stops being reliable.

Finding 04 · Fleet

The e-bike is the default bike.

Across every month measured, e-bikes carry between 72% and 77% of all rides, climbing to 78.9% by 7pm on a Friday. Classic bikes are the minority option — which inverts the usual framing, where the e-bike is the scarce upgrade you hope to find.

The practical consequence: a station showing "8 bikes available" tells you very little until you know the split. Six classics and two e-bikes is a materially worse station than the reverse for most riders here, and the aggregate count hides that completely.

Finding 05 · Traffic

It's bridge traffic, not commuting.

The busiest station pairs are all short hops that dead-end at a bridge ramp. N 6 St & Bedford Ave to S 2 St & Kent Ave — the Bedford L stop to the Williamsburg Bridge landing — runs 2,662 rides in four months, with 2,392 coming back the other way. On the Manhattan side, Henry St & Grand St and Norfolk St & Broome St trade the same way.

Limits

What this data cannot tell you.

  • This is satisfied demand only. A ride that never happened because no bike was there leaves no record. A station that looks quiet may be starved rather than unpopular — and the deepest drains are precisely where that undercount bites hardest, because they hit empty and stop generating trips.
  • Rebalancing is invisible. Trucks move bikes all day and file no trip. So accumulated pressure is what a station would do unaided — read it as "how much resupply this needs to stay usable," never as a live bike count.
  • Nothing here is dock availability. Trips describe flow, not level. Only the live 5-minute polling can say whether there is a bike in the rack right now — which is exactly why both halves of this project exist.
  • Four months, one season. May–August. No winter, and no rain analysis yet; weather effects need enough wet days to contrast against dry ones.
